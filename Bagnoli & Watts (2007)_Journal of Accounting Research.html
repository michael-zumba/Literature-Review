1. Literature Review Components¶

  • Title: Financial Reporting and Supplemental Voluntary Disclosures
  • Journal: Journal of Accounting Research
  • Authors: Mark Bagnoli and Susan G. Watts
  • In-text Citation Format: Bagnoli & Watts (2007)
  • Overview:
    The paper examines how mandatory financial disclosures interact with managers' voluntary supplemental disclosures when the manager's private information complements (rather than substitutes for) mandatory reports.
  • Key Contribution to Field:
  • Challenges the standard result that mandatory and voluntary disclosures are substitutes.
  • Shows that voluntary disclosures depend on whether mandatory reports contain good or bad news.
  • Provides new empirical predictions about the likelihood and market reaction to voluntary disclosures.
  • Policy/Practical Implications:
  • Regulatory changes to mandatory disclosures can have unintended consequences on voluntary disclosures.
  • Highlights the importance of considering how managers interpret and supplement financial reports.
  • Methodology:
  • Theoretical model using a signaling framework with joint normality assumptions.
  • Manager observes the variance (permanent vs. transitory components) of a financial report item and decides whether to disclose it.
  • Results:
  • Managers disclose small (large) variances when financial reports contain good (bad) news to amplify (mitigate) market reactions.
  • Probability of voluntary disclosure increases with the extremity of the news in financial reports.
  • Discussion:
  • Voluntary disclosures are strategic tools to influence market perceptions of financial report content.
  • The interaction between mandatory and voluntary disclosures depends on the covariance structure of financial items.
  • Research Gaps/Areas for Improvement:
  • Model assumes truthful disclosure; future work could explore cheap-talk or misreporting incentives.
  • Empirical validation of the model's predictions is needed.
  • Paper's Strengths and Weaknesses:
  • Strengths: Novel theoretical insights, clear testable implications.
  • Weaknesses: Limited empirical validation; assumes cost of disclosure is exogenous.
  • Limitations and Future Research:
  • Extend to endogenous financial reporting quality or disclosure costs.
  • Test predictions using earnings announcements and concurrent disclosures.

2. Variables Summary¶

  • Dependent Variable(s):
  • Firm value (v)
    • Measurement: Expected liquidation value conditional on financial reports and voluntary disclosures.
    • Economic Significance: Market price reaction to disclosures.
    • Data Source: Market prices.
  • Independent Variable(s):
  • Financial report items (x₁, x₂, e.g., revenues, expenses)
    • Measurement: Reported values with permanent/transitory components.
    • Data Source: Firm financial statements.
  • Manager's private information (σ₁₁, variance of x₁)
    • Measurement: Precision of financial item x₁.
    • Data Source: Manager's internal information.
  • Control Variables:
  • Market expectations (µ₁, µ₂)
    • Justification: Baseline for evaluating surprises.
    • Data Source: Analyst forecasts or historical averages.

3. Methodology Summary¶

  • Analytical Methods:
  • Bayesian updating under joint normality.
  • Equilibrium disclosure strategies using threshold rules.
  • Model Equations:
  • Market price with disclosure:
    E[v | x, σ₁₁] = µ_v + β₁(x₁ − µ₁) + β₂(x₂ − µ₂)
  • Market price without disclosure:
    E[v | x, σ₁₁ ∈ N] = µ_v + Eβ₁ | x, σ₁₁ ∈ N + Eβ₂ | x, σ₁₁ ∈ N
  • Identification Strategy:
  • Manager discloses if the net benefit (price impact minus cost c) is positive.
  • Robustness Tests:
  • Examines cases with correlated transitory components (σ₁₂ ≠ 0).
  • Data Sampling:
  • Theoretical model; no empirical data.

  • N/A (analytical model).

4. Theory Summary¶

  • Name of Theory: Voluntary Disclosure Theory (with complementary information).
  • Origin of Theory: Extends Verrecchia (1983), Dye (1985).
  • Description of Theory:
  • Managers voluntarily disclose private information to influence market interpretation of mandatory reports.
  • Application in Study:
  • Disclosures depend on whether financial reports contain good/bad news.
  • Empirical Validation:
  • Predicts higher ERCs for good news with voluntary disclosures.
  • Competing Theories Considered:
  • Standard substitute disclosure models (e.g., Verrecchia 1983).
