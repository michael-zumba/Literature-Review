1. Literature Review Components¶

  • Title: Diversity Washing
  • Journal: Journal of Accounting Research
  • Authors: Andrew C. Baker, David F. Larcker, Charles G. McClure, Durgesh Saraph, Edward M. Watts
  • In-text Citation Format: Baker et al. (2024)
  • Overview: This paper investigates whether U.S. publicly traded corporations opportunistically use voluntary disclosures about their commitments to employee diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) to misrepresent their actual hiring practices.
  • Key Contribution to Field: Provides large-sample empirical evidence on discrepancies between firms' external DEI stances and their actual diversity metrics, coining the term "diversity washing." Highlights how such firms attract higher ESG ratings and investment despite poor DEI outcomes.
  • Policy/Practical Implications: Calls for standardized, mandatory DEI disclosures and stronger regulatory enforcement to curb misleading ESG claims. Raises concerns about misallocation of ESG-focused capital.
  • Methodology: Uses textual analysis of SEC filings (10-Ks, 8-Ks, proxy statements) to measure DEI discussion, paired with workforce diversity data from Revelio Labs. Employs regression analyses, Poisson models, and validation tests (e.g., EEOC violations, ESG ratings).
  • Results: Firms with high DEI discussion but low diversity ("diversity washers") face more discrimination violations and negative human-capital events. They receive higher ESG ratings and attract more ESG-focused investors.
  • Discussion: Diversity washing is linked to vague language, forward-looking statements without follow-through, and broader ESG misrepresentation (e.g., policies without targets).
  • Research Gaps/Areas for Improvement: Limited to U.S. firms; reliance on Revelio Labs' diversity metrics (potential white-collar bias). Future research could explore global contexts or alternative diversity data sources.
  • Paper's Strengths and Weaknesses:
    • Strengths: Novel large-sample evidence; robust validation tests; interdisciplinary relevance (accounting, finance, ESG).
    • Weaknesses: Endogeneity concerns (diversity washing is self-selected); no causal identification.
  • Limitations and Future Research: Suggests examining non-U.S. markets, longitudinal effects of regulation, and investor reactions to diversity-washing exposure.

2. Variables Summary¶

  • Dependent Variable(s):
    • DEI Words (Aggregate): Count of DEI-related terms in SEC filings.
    • Measurement: Dictionary-based textual analysis.
    • Source: WRDS SEC EDGAR.
    • ESG Ratings: Scores from Refinitiv/Sustainalytics.
    • Measurement: Normalized ratings (0–100).
    • Source: Refinitiv, Sustainalytics.
  • Independent Variable(s):
    • % Diversity: Fraction of female/non-white employees.
    • Measurement: Revelio Labs' workforce data.
    • Source: Revelio Labs.
    • Diversity Washer: Binary (1 if DEI discussion > diversity percentile).
  • Control Variables: Firm size, profitability, volatility, industry/year fixed effects.
  • Other Variables:
    • EEOC Violations: Discrimination penalties (GoodJobsFirst).
    • Forward-Looking Terms: Count in DEI sentences (Bozanic et al. 2018 dictionary).

3. Methodology Summary¶

  • Analytical Methods: Poisson regressions (DEI word counts), OLS (ESG ratings), logit (violations).
  • Model Equations:
    • DEI Words: Poisson(β0 + β1 Diversity + β2 Controls + FE).
    • ESG Ratings: OLS(β0 + β1 Diversity Washer + β2 Controls + FE).
  • Identification Strategy: Within-year percentile ranks to isolate abnormal DEI discussion.
  • Robustness Tests: Alternative timing, dictionary expansions, entropy balancing.
  • Endogeneity Tests: Future diversity hiring analyses (no aspirational improvements).
  • Data Sampling: U.S. public firms, 2008–2021 (N ≈ 47,334 firm-years).
  • Software/Tools: Stata, Python (sentenceBERT, ChatGPT for topic labeling).

4. Theory Summary¶

  • Name of Theory: Agency Theory (Jensen & Meckling 1976).
  • Origin: Corporate governance literature.
  • Description: Managers may misrepresent DEI to attract ESG investors, leveraging information asymmetry.
  • Application in Study: Diversity washing as a managerial opportunism tool.
  • Empirical Validation: Supported by higher ESG ratings/investment despite poor DEI outcomes.
  • Competing Theories: Stakeholder theory (not supported; diversity washers harm employees/investors).

5. Data Source Summary¶

  • Dependent Variables:
    • DEI Words: SEC EDGAR.
    • Diversity Metrics: Revelio Labs (U.S. employees).
  • Independent/Control Variables: Compustat, CRSP.
  • Temporal Coverage: 2008–2021.
  • Sample Size: ~5,000 firms, 47,334 firm-years.
  • Data Limitations: Revelio data may skew toward white-collar roles; no international coverage.

6. Key Empirical Findings¶

  • Statistical vs Economic Significance:
    • Diversity washers have 12% higher ESG ratings (p < 0.01) but 0.5% lower future diversity (p < 0.05).
  • Magnitude Interpretation: 1 SD increase in diversity washing → 19% more ESG fund ownership.
  • Cross-study Comparison: Aligns with Liang et al. (2022) on selective diversity disclosures.
