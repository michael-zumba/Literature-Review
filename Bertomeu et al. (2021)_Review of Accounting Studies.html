1. Literature Review Components¶

  • Title: Voluntary versus mandatory disclosure
  • Journal: Review of Accounting Studies
  • Authors: Jeremy Bertomeu, Igor Vaysman, Wenjie Xue
  • In-text Citation Format: Bertomeu et al. (2021)
  • Overview: The paper develops a theory of asymmetries between voluntary and mandatory disclosure, showing that efficiently designed mandatory policies substitute for excessive voluntary disclosures. The optimal policy mandates disclosure of bad news below a threshold while allowing voluntary disclosure of good news above another threshold.
  • Key Contribution to Field:
  • Theoretical justification for asymmetric (conservative) reporting standards.
  • Demonstrates that mandatory disclosure of bad news reduces socially excessive voluntary disclosure of intermediate news.
  • Extends the analysis to settings with real effects (investment, liquidations, lemons markets).
  • Policy/Practical Implications:
  • Supports conservatism in accounting standards (e.g., impairment rules).
  • Explains why mandatory disclosures (e.g., SEC filings) often trigger negative market reactions.
  • Methodology:
  • Mechanism design model with costly voluntary disclosure and mandatory disclosure thresholds.
  • Analyzes equilibrium under log-concave distributions (e.g., normal).
  • Extensions include convex payoffs, liquidation decisions, and adverse selection.
  • Results:
  • Efficient policies mandate disclosure of sufficiently unfavorable news (θ < mode < τ).
  • Mandatory disclosure increases with information precision (σ↓ → θ*↑).
  • In lemons markets, mandatory disclosure mitigates adverse selection.
  • Discussion:
  • Challenges symmetric recognition under IFRS by highlighting market benefits of conservatism.
  • Links to empirical findings (e.g., negative reactions to 8-K filings; Noh et al. 2019 on substitution effects).
  • Research Gaps/Areas for Improvement:
  • Assumes perfect enforcement; future work could explore enforcement costs.
  • Limited to verifiable information; "soft" news is excluded.
  • Paper's Strengths and Weaknesses:
  • Strengths: Generalizable framework with multiple applications (investment, liquidation, lemons markets).
  • Weaknesses: Linear pricing in baseline model; convex payoffs are an extension.
  • Limitations and Future Research:
  • Endogenizing disclosure costs (e.g., proprietary costs).
  • Testing predictions empirically (e.g., cross-country variation in conservatism).

2. Variables Summary¶

  • Dependent Variable(s):
  • Disclosure choice: Binary (voluntary/mandatory/nondisclosure).
  • Market price (P): Linear in baseline; convex in extensions.
  • Independent Variable(s):
  • News (x): Posterior expectation of asset value, drawn from a log-concave distribution (e.g., normal).
  • Disclosure cost (c): Fixed cost for voluntary/mandatory disclosure.
  • Control Variables:
  • Verification cost (h(θ)): Cost to confirm nondisclosure compliance (Section 5.1).
  • Liquidation value (δx): Opportunity cost of retaining assets (Section 4.2).

3. Methodology Summary¶

  • Analytical Methods:
  • Equilibrium analysis with threshold strategies.
  • Comparative statics using implicit function theorem.
  • Model Equations:
  • Voluntary disclosure threshold:
    [
    \tau_\theta - c = E(x | x \notin D_m, x \leq \tau_\theta).
    ]
  • Optimal mandatory threshold (Eq. 3.3):
    [
    \frac{F(\tau_{\theta^}) - F(\theta^)}{\tau_{\theta^} - \theta^} = f(\tau_{\theta^*}).
    ]
  • Identification Strategy:
  • Uses single-peakedness of distributions to ensure uniqueness.
  • Robustness Tests:
  • Log-concavity ensures global optimality.
  • Extensions with convex payoffs, liquidation, and lemons markets.
  • Data Sampling: Theoretical model; no empirical data.

4. Theory Summary¶

  • Name of Theory: Costly voluntary disclosure with mandatory regulation.
  • Origin of Theory: Extends Jovanovic (1982), Verrecchia (1983).
  • Description: Firms optimally disclose news to maximize value, accounting for disclosure costs and regulatory thresholds.
  • Application in Study:
  • Mandatory disclosure mitigates negative externality of excessive voluntary disclosure.
  • Empirical Validation: Consistent with negative market reactions to mandatory disclosures (Lerman & Livnat 2010).
  • Competing Theories:
  • Full unraveling (Milgrom 1981) vs. partial disclosure here.
  • Competing explanations for conservatism (e.g., contracting vs. market efficiency).

6. Key Empirical Findings¶

  • Statistical vs. Economic Significance:
  • Mandatory disclosures are statistically associated with negative news (θ* < mode).
  • Economic significance depends on disclosure cost (c) and distribution shape.
  • Cross-study Comparison:
  • Contrasts with Heinle & Verrecchia (2015): Focus on ex-ante commitment.
  • Differs from Suijs (2005): Bad news disclosure due to regulation, not verification costs.
