1. Literature Review Components¶

  • Title: The Role of Disclosure and Information Intermediaries in an Unregulated Capital Market: Evidence from Initial Coin Offerings
  • Journal: Journal of Accounting Research
  • Authors: Thomas Bourveau, Emmanuel T. De George, Atif Ellahie, and Daniele Macciocchi
  • In-text Citation Format: Bourveau et al. (2022)
  • Overview: This paper examines voluntary disclosure practices and the role of information intermediaries in the unregulated Initial Coin Offering (ICO) market, using a sample of 2,113 ICOs from 2014 to 2018.
  • Key Contribution to Field: Provides empirical evidence on how voluntary disclosure and information intermediaries facilitate capital raising in unregulated markets, addressing gaps in understanding alternative financing mechanisms.
  • Policy/Practical Implications: Highlights the potential for market-based mechanisms (e.g., ratings platforms) to substitute for formal regulation in emerging capital markets.
  • Methodology: Cross-sectional OLS regressions, hand-collected disclosure data, and principal component analysis.
  • Results: Higher disclosure levels are associated with successful capital raising, especially when complemented by internal governance (e.g., token vesting) or external scrutiny (e.g., ratings).
  • Discussion: Voluntary disclosures in ICOs resemble IPO prospectuses but lack financial/risk disclosures. Information intermediaries enhance disclosure credibility.
  • Research Gaps/Areas for Improvement: Limited causal inferences due to endogeneity; future research could explore dynamic disclosure practices post-ICO.
  • Paper's Strengths and Weaknesses:
    • Strengths: Novel setting (unregulated market), comprehensive disclosure taxonomy, large international sample.
    • Weaknesses: Associational results, limited ex-post performance data.
  • Limitations and Future Research: Suggests examining long-term ICO performance and regulatory evolution in crypto-markets.

2. Variables Summary¶

  • Dependent Variable(s):
    • Raised: Binary indicator for successful capital raising (1 if funds raised exceed soft cap).
    • USD Raised: Log of funds raised (in USD).
    • Failure: Binary indicator for inactive projects/delisted tokens.
    • 6mth Return: Post-listing market-adjusted returns.
  • Independent Variable(s):
    • Disclosure Index: Aggregate of 10 disclosure items (e.g., white paper length, team bios, source code release).
    • Pre-ICO Rating: Normalized rating from ICObench (0–1).
  • Control Variables:
    • Team Size, Utility Token (dummy), Soft Cap Requirement, Bitcoin Momentum, country/industry/quarter fixed effects.

3. Methodology Summary¶

  • Analytical Methods: OLS regressions with clustered standard errors (country-quarter).
  • Model Equations:
    • Capital raising: Raised_i = β0 + β1 Disclosure_i + γ'Controls_i + θ_r + λ_j + τ_t + ε_i.
    • Ratings validation: Failure/6mth Return ~ Pre-ICO Rating + Controls.
  • Identification Strategy: Exploits cross-sectional variation in disclosure and ratings coverage.
  • Robustness Tests: Alternative disclosure measures (principal components), matched samples for rated vs. unrated ICOs.
  • Data Sampling: 2,113 ICOs (2014–2018), global coverage, excludes ICOs without white papers.
  • Software/Tools Used: Stata (regressions), Python (text analysis).

4. Theory Summary¶

  • Name of Theory: Voluntary Disclosure Theory, Agency Theory.
  • Origin: Crawford and Sobel (1982), Jensen and Meckling (1976).
  • Description: Firms disclose information to reduce asymmetry; credibility hinges on governance/incentives.
  • Application in Study: ICOs use disclosures to signal quality; vesting/ratings mitigate moral hazard.
  • Empirical Validation: Disclosure positively correlates with funding success, especially with governance/ratings.
  • Competing Theories: Stewardship theory (not supported; team disclosure aligns with agency predictions).

5. Data Source Summary¶

  • Primary Sources:
    • White papers (hand-collected), ICObench (ratings), Coinmarketcap, GitHub (code), social media.
  • Temporal Coverage: March 2014 – October 2018.
  • Sample Selection: ICOs with white papers; excludes scams/inactive projects.
  • Sample Size: 2,113 ICOs (1,022 with funding data).
  • Data Limitations: Survivorship bias (failed ICOs may lack disclosures), unverifiable self-reported data.

6. Key Empirical Findings¶

  • Statistical Significance: Disclosure Index coefficient = 0.330*** (t = 9.29).
  • Economic Significance: 1 SD increase in disclosure → 10% higher probability of raising capital.
  • Cross-study Comparison: Consistent with IPO literature (e.g., Leone et al. 2007) but unique to unregulated settings.

7. Replication Materials¶

  • Data Availability: From ICObench, Coinmarketcap (subscription required).

8. Discipline-Specific Enhancements¶

  • Corporate Governance Mechanism: Token vesting (26% of ICOs) strengthens disclosure credibility.
  • Financial Regulation Context: Highlights SEC’s Howey Rule challenges for crypto-tokens.
  • Market Microstructure: Analyzes post-ICO liquidity via exchange listings.
