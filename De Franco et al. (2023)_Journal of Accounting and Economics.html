1. Literature Review Components¶

  • Title: Fixed Income Conference Calls
  • Journal: Journal of Accounting and Economics
  • Authors: Gus De Franco, Thomas Shohfi, Da Xu, Zhiwei (Vivi) Zhu
  • In-text Citation Format: De Franco et al. (2023)
  • Overview: Examines the determinants and informational role of fixed income (FI) conference calls, a voluntary disclosure tool targeting debt investors.
  • Key Contribution to Field:
  • First study to analyze FI calls as a distinct disclosure channel for debt investors.
  • Documents how FI calls address debt-equity conflicts and cater to debtholders' unique informational needs.
  • Provides empirical evidence on market reactions to FI calls in bond and CDS markets.
  • Policy/Practical Implications:
  • Highlights the importance of tailored disclosures for debt investors, especially for firms with high debt, weak information environments, or negative performance.
  • Suggests FI calls can reduce information asymmetry and improve debt market efficiency.
  • Methodology:
  • Combines hand-collected FI call data (2001–2020) with regression analysis, content analysis (textual dictionaries), and event studies (bond/CDS reactions).
  • Results:
  • FI calls are more likely for firms with higher debt, no credit ratings, foreign operations, or losses.
  • Content analysis reveals FI calls emphasize debt-specific topics (e.g., covenants, liquidity) and have a more negative tone than earnings calls.
  • Bond/CDS markets react significantly to FI calls, indicating informational value.
  • Discussion:
  • FI calls complement earnings calls by addressing debtholders’ asymmetric payoff concerns and longer-term horizons.
  • Private firms use FI calls as a substitute for earnings calls, while public firms use them as supplements.
  • Research Gaps/Areas for Improvement:
  • Limited sample of FI call transcripts (29.8% of total calls).
  • Potential survivorship bias (unpublicized FI calls excluded).
  • Paper's Strengths and Weaknesses:
  • Strengths: Novel focus on FI calls, robust multi-method design, practitioner interviews.
  • Weaknesses: Sample skewed toward U.S./U.K./Canadian firms; CDS/bond data limited to public firms.
  • Limitations and Future Research:
  • Explore cross-country regulatory differences (e.g., Regulation FD applicability).
  • Investigate FI calls’ role during crises (e.g., COVID-19).

2. Variables Summary¶

  • Dependent Variables:
  • FI Call (Indicator): Equals 1 if firm holds an FI call in year t (Source: S&P Capital IQ, Bloomberg).
  • Bond Trading Volume: Daily bond trades scaled by outstanding debt face value (Source: TRACE).
  • CDS Spread Change: Absolute daily change in 5-year CDS spreads (Source: Markit).
  • Independent Variables:
  • Debt-to-Assets: Total debt / total assets (Source: Compustat).
  • Number of Debt Types: Count of debt instruments (e.g., bonds, loans) (Source: S&P Capital IQ).
  • Rated (Indicator): Equals 1 if firm has a credit rating (Source: S&P).
  • Loss (Indicator): Equals 1 if net income < 0 (Source: Compustat).
  • Control Variables:
  • Firm size, sales growth, industry indicators (e.g., financial, high-tech) (Source: Compustat/SIC codes).

3. Methodology Summary¶

  • Analytical Methods:
  • Logit models for FI call determinants.
  • Textual analysis using custom dictionaries (fixed income, financial, short-term words).
  • Event studies (3-day windows) for bond/CDS reactions.
  • Identification Strategy:
  • Exploits cross-sectional variation in firm-debt characteristics (e.g., maturity, rating status).
  • Robustness Tests:
  • Matched samples (by industry/size), alternative variable definitions.
  • Data Sampling:
  • 1,612 FI calls (424 firms; 2001–2020).
  • Bond/CDS tests: 304–365 events after excluding confounders.
  • Software/Tools Used: Stata, textual parsing scripts.

4. Theory Summary¶

  • Name of Theory: Agency Theory
  • Origin: Jensen & Meckling (1976)
  • Description: Conflicts between managers and debtholders arise from wealth transfer risks (e.g., asset substitution).
  • Application in Study: FI calls mitigate agency conflicts by providing debt-specific monitoring information.
  • Competing Theories: Stewardship theory (not supported; FI calls emphasize asymmetric information needs).

5. Data Source Summary¶

  • Dependent Variables:
  • FI calls: S&P Capital IQ, Bloomberg.
  • Bond trades: TRACE.
  • Independent/Control Variables: Compustat, S&P Capital IQ.
  • Temporal Coverage: 2001–2020.
  • Sample Size: 878 firm-years (FI calls); 304–365 for event studies.
  • Data Limitations:
  • Transcripts available for only 29.8% of FI calls.
  • CDS data limited to firms with traded swaps.

6. Key Empirical Findings¶

  • Economic Significance:
  • FI calls increase bond trading volume by 21% ($10M daily).
  • CDS spreads rise by 2.08% during FI call windows.
  • Cross-study Comparison:
  • Similar to Kimbrough & Louis (2011) for M&A calls, but tailored to debt markets.

7. Replication Materials¶

  • Data Availability: Subscription required (Compustat, TRACE, Markit).
  • Code Repository: Not disclosed.

8. Discipline-Specific Enhancements¶

  • GAAP/IFRS: FI calls often discuss covenant compliance (GAAP-based ratios).
  • Corporate Governance: Highlights Treasurer/CAO roles in debt disclosure.
  • Financial Regulation: Questions Regulation FD applicability to FI calls.
