1. Literature Review Components¶

  • Title: Institutional Blockholders and Voluntary Disclosure
  • Journal: European Accounting Review
  • Authors: Xiaochi Ge, Pawel Bilinski, Arthur Kraft
  • In-text Citation Format: Ge et al. (2021)
  • Overview: Examines how concentrated institutional ownership affects firms' voluntary disclosure practices, including management forecasts, conference calls, and 8-K filings.
  • Key Contribution to Field:
    • Documents a negative relationship between institutional blockholdings (≥5% ownership) and voluntary disclosure, contrasting prior findings that average institutional ownership increases disclosure.
    • Identifies two channels: (1) blockholders substitute private for public information access, and (2) passive blockholders reduce demand for disclosure.
  • Policy/Practical Implications: Highlights the trade-off between blockholder monitoring and corporate transparency, relevant for regulators assessing disclosure policies.
  • Methodology:
    • Uses panel data (2001–2015) with firm-quarter observations (N=104,765).
    • Employs logit/Poisson regressions, instrumental variables (Russell 3000 index inclusion), and robustness tests (e.g., Herfindahl index, board seats).
  • Results:
    • A 1 SD increase in blockholdings reduces management forecast likelihood by 16.6%.
    • Effects are stronger when blockholders hold board seats or are passive investors.
  • Discussion: Challenges the assumption that all institutional investors uniformly demand more disclosure; blockholders’ concentrated stakes alter incentives.
  • Research Gaps/Areas for Improvement: Limited to U.S. firms; cross-country comparisons could generalize findings.
  • Paper's Strengths and Weaknesses:
    • Strengths: Robust identification (IV, fixed effects), multiple disclosure measures.
    • Weaknesses: Potential residual endogeneity; passive blockholder classification may omit quasi-indexers.
  • Limitations and Future Research: Suggests examining blockholder heterogeneity (e.g., activist vs. passive) and non-U.S. settings.

2. Variables Summary¶

  • Dependent Variables:
    • MF_occur: Binary indicator for management forecasts (I/B/E/S).
    • MF_items: Count of forecasted items (e.g., EPS, revenue).
    • Conference calls: Binary indicator (Thomson Reuters StreetEvents).
    • 8-K filings: Count of voluntary filings (SEC).
  • Independent Variables:
    • Block: % shares held by institutional blockholders (≥5%; 13-F filings).
    • HHI: Herfindahl index of institutional ownership concentration.
  • Control Variables:
    • Firm size, leverage, ROA, analyst coverage, board independence, etc. (Compustat/CRSP/BoardEx).

3. Methodology Summary¶

  • Analytical Methods: Logit/Poisson regressions, 2SLS IV.
  • Model Equations:
    • Logit: P(MF_occur) = α0 + α1Block + α2IO + Controls + FE + ε.
    • IV: Russell 3000 inclusion instruments for blockholdings.
  • Identification Strategy:
    • Exploits Russell index reconstitutions for exogenous variation.
    • Tests cross-sectional heterogeneity (e.g., analyst coverage, fiscal Q4 busyness).
  • Robustness Tests: Alternative blockholder definitions, fixed effects, changes regressions.
  • Data Sampling: 2001–2015; excludes financials; winsorized at 0.1%/99.9%.

4. Theory Summary¶

  • Name of Theory: Agency Theory (Jensen & Meckling, 1976).
  • Application in Study: Blockholders mitigate agency costs but reduce public disclosure due to private access.
  • Competing Theories: Stewardship theory (not supported; passive blockholders discourage disclosure).

5. Data Source Summary¶

  • Sources: Compustat, CRSP, I/B/E/S, BoardEx, Thomson Reuters, SEC.
  • Temporal Coverage: 2001–2015.
  • Sample Size: 104,765 firm-quarters.
  • Limitations: Survivorship bias; U.S.-centric.

6. Key Empirical Findings¶

  • Economic Significance: Blockholdings reduce disclosure likelihood more than non-blockholdings increase it.
  • Cross-study Comparison: Contrasts with Boone & White (2015), who find passive ownership increases disclosure.

7. Replication Materials¶

  • Data Availability: Subscription-based (Compustat, BoardEx).
  • Code Repository: Not disclosed.

8. Discipline-Specific Enhancements¶

  • Corporate Governance: Blockholder board seats amplify disclosure reduction.
  • Financial Regulation: Highlights unintended consequences of ownership concentration on transparency.
