1. Literature Review Components¶

  • Title: A new era of voluntary disclosure? Empirical evidence on how employee postings on social media relate to future corporate disclosures
  • Journal: Accounting, Organizations and Society
  • Authors: Jeffrey Hales, James R. Moon Jr., Laura A. Swenson
  • In-text Citation Format: Hales et al. (2018)
  • Overview: The study examines whether employee reviews on Glassdoor.com, a social media platform, contain information predictive of future corporate disclosures (both mandatory and voluntary).
  • Key Contribution to Field: Provides empirical evidence that rank-and-file employees’ social media postings can serve as an alternative disclosure channel, challenging the traditional view that voluntary disclosures are solely managed by executives.
  • Policy/Practical Implications: Suggests that employee opinions on social media can inform investors, analysts, and auditors, reducing information asymmetry. Regulators may need to consider the role of such platforms in disclosure ecosystems.
  • Methodology: Uses a sample of ~150,000 Glassdoor reviews from S&P 1500 firms. Combines factor analysis (to extract latent outlook measures) with regression models to test associations between employee outlook and future disclosures.
  • Results: Employee outlook predicts future income statement metrics (e.g., sales, gross margins), transitory items (e.g., restructuring charges), earnings surprises, and management forecast news.
  • Discussion: Employees’ collective opinions chip away at management’s exclusive control over disclosures. Results are robust to controls for firm characteristics and other review components.
  • Research Gaps/Areas for Improvement: Limited to U.S. firms; qualitative review content (e.g., text) was excluded. Future research could explore cross-country differences or integrate NLP for text analysis.
  • Paper's Strengths and Weaknesses:
    • Strengths: Large dataset; novel focus on employee-driven disclosures; robust methodology.
    • Weaknesses: Self-selection bias in reviews; inability to pinpoint exact timing of former employees’ departures.
  • Limitations and Future Research: Suggests examining non-U.S. contexts, longer-term horizons, and mechanisms linking employee sentiment to performance.

2. Variables Summary¶

  • Dependent Variables:
    • Future Performance: Sales, gross margins (GM), operating income (OpInc), net income (Income), scaled by assets.
    • Data Source: Compustat.
    • Transitory Items: Goodwill impairments (GWImp), write-downs (Writedown), restructuring charges (Restructure).
    • Data Source: Compustat.
    • Earnings Surprise: Difference between actual earnings and analyst consensus (IBES).
    • Management Forecast News: Difference between management forecasts and analyst consensus (IBES).
  • Independent Variables:
    • Explicit Outlook: Employee-rated firm outlook (positive/neutral/negative).
    • Latent Outlook (OutlookFactor): Derived from factor analysis of Glassdoor review components.
    • Data Source: Glassdoor.com.
  • Control Variables:
    • Firm size, age, profitability (ROA), stock returns, SG&A ratio, workforce growth, return volatility, book-to-market ratio, analyst following, forecast dispersion.
    • Justification: Standard controls for firm performance, risk, and information environment.
    • Data Sources: Compustat, CRSP, IBES.

3. Methodology Summary¶

  • Analytical Methods: Panel regressions with firm/quarter fixed effects; factor analysis (principal factoring with promax rotation).
  • Model Equations:
    • Future Performance:
      ( \text{Performance}{t+1} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{Outlook}_t + \beta + \epsilon ). }
    • Earnings Surprise:
      ( \text{EarningsSurprise}{t+1} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{Outlook}_t + \beta + \epsilon ). }
  • Identification Strategy: Uses time-stamped reviews to establish temporal precedence. Controls for past performance and firm characteristics.
  • Robustness Tests:
    • Excludes industries with high review volume (Business Services, Retail).
    • Tests alternative factor extraction methods (unrotated factors).
  • Endogeneity Tests: Controls for lagged performance and firm fixed effects.
  • Data Sampling: 158,352 reviews (1,265 S&P 1500 firms; 2012–2015).
  • Software/Tools Used: Python (web scraping), Stata (regressions).

4. Theory Summary¶

  • Name of Theory: Agency Theory, Information Asymmetry.
  • Origin of Theory: Jensen & Meckling (1976).
  • Description of Theory: Conflicts arise between managers (agents) and shareholders (principals) due to information asymmetry.
  • Application in Study: Employees’ social media posts reduce asymmetry by providing independent insights into firm prospects.
  • Empirical Validation: Results align with theory—employee outlook predicts future disclosures, supporting the idea that non-executives hold valuable information.
  • Competing Theories Considered: Stewardship theory (not directly tested).

5. Data Source Summary¶

  • Dependent Variables: Compustat (financials), IBES (earnings/forecasts).
  • Independent Variables: Glassdoor.com (employee reviews).
  • Control Variables: CRSP (returns), Compustat (firm characteristics).
  • Temporal Coverage: 2012–2015.
  • Sample Selection Criteria: S&P 1500 firms with Glassdoor reviews; excludes incomplete reviews and former employees (in main tests).
  • Sample Size: 158,352 reviews.
  • Data Cleaning Procedures: Winsorized at 1%/99%; matched firm identifiers manually.
  • Data Limitations: Survivorship bias (only firms with reviews); self-reported tenure/employment status.

6. Key Empirical Findings¶

  • Statistical vs. Economic Significance:
    • Outlook: 1 SD increase → 0.5% higher ROA (economically meaningful).
  • Magnitude Interpretation: Positive outlook reviews associated with 2.4% higher sales/assets.
  • Cross-study Comparison: Consistent with Babenko & Sen (2015) on employee information advantages.

7. Replication Materials¶

  • Data Availability: Glassdoor data requires subscription; Compustat/CRSP/IBES via standard vendors.
  • Code Repository: Not mentioned.

8. Discipline-Specific Enhancements¶

  • Corporate Governance Mechanism: Highlights erosion of management’s disclosure monopoly.
  • Financial Regulation Context: Suggests social media platforms may warrant regulatory attention as disclosure channels.
