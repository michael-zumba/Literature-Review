1. Literature Review Components¶

  • Title: Readability of narrative disclosures, and corporate liquidity and payout policies
  • Journal: International Review of Financial Analysis
  • Authors: Mostafa Monzur Hasan, Ahsan Habib
  • In-text Citation Format: Hasan & Habib (2020)
  • Overview: Examines the relationship between the readability of narrative disclosures in 10-K reports and corporate cash holdings/payout policies (dividends and stock repurchases).
  • Key Contribution to Field:
  • First systematic evidence linking 10-K readability to internal corporate financial policies (cash holdings and payouts).
  • Extends literature on textual analysis by showing how disclosure complexity affects managerial decisions.
  • Policy/Practical Implications:
  • Highlights the economic costs of poor readability (e.g., higher cash holdings, reduced payouts).
  • Suggests improving readability can mitigate agency costs and enhance shareholder value.
  • Methodology:
  • Uses OLS, Logit, and Tobit regressions with two-way clustered standard errors.
  • Sample: 81,723 firm-years (cash holdings) and 70,400 firm-years (payouts) of U.S. firms (1994–2017).
  • Primary readability measure: Fog Index (FOG); robustness checks with SMOG, BOG, and file size.
  • Results:
  • Cash Holdings: Less readable disclosures correlate with higher cash holdings (8.19–10.22% increase per 1 SD rise in FOG).
  • Payouts: Less readable firms pay fewer dividends (14.63% decrease) and repurchase fewer stocks (5.85% decrease).
  • Cross-sectional effects: Stronger for firms with weak governance, high financing constraints, and refinancing risks.
  • Discussion:
  • Supports agency and precautionary motives for cash holdings.
  • Less readable disclosures enable managerial opportunism and increase external financing costs.
  • Research Gaps/Areas for Improvement:
  • Limited to U.S. firms; international comparisons needed.
  • Potential omitted variables (e.g., managerial talent).
  • Paper's Strengths and Weaknesses:
  • Strengths: Large sample, robust endogeneity controls (2SLS), multiple readability measures.
  • Weaknesses: Reliance on Fog Index may not capture all dimensions of readability.
  • Limitations and Future Research:
  • Explore non-linear effects of readability.
  • Investigate readability’s role in other corporate policies (e.g., investment efficiency).

2. Variables Summary¶

  • Dependent Variables:
  • Cash Holdings:
    • CASH/TA: Cash/assets ratio.
    • CASH/NA: Cash/net assets.
    • Data Source: Compustat.
  • Payouts:
    • DIV_D: Dividend payer dummy.
    • REP/TA: Repurchases/assets.
    • Data Source: Compustat.
  • Independent Variable:
  • FOG: Fog Index (higher = less readable). Data Source: WRDS SEC Analytics Suite.
  • Control Variables:
  • Firm size (SIZE), leverage (LEV), R&D, governance (INST), etc.
  • Justification: Standard controls in corporate finance literature.

3. Methodology Summary¶

  • Analytical Methods: OLS, Logit, Tobit, 2SLS.
  • Model Equations:
  • Cash holdings:
    [ \text{CASH}{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{FOG} ]} + \text{Controls} + \text{Fixed Effects} + \epsilon_{it
  • Payouts (Logit):
    [ \text{Prob(DIV_D}{it} = 1) = \text{Logit}(\beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{FOG}) ]} + \text{Controls
  • Identification Strategy:
  • Lagged readability to address reverse causality.
  • Instrumental variable (state-year average readability) for 2SLS.
  • Robustness Tests:
  • Alternative readability measures (SMOG, BOG, file size).
  • Additional controls (governance, financial constraints).
  • Data Sampling:
  • Excludes financial/utility firms; winsorizes top/bottom 1%.
  • Software/Tools: Stata (two-way clustering).

4. Theory Summary¶

  • Name of Theory: Agency Theory, Precautionary Motive.
  • Origin: Jensen & Meckling (1976), Almeida et al. (2004).
  • Description:
  • Agency Theory: Managers hoard cash to avoid scrutiny.
  • Precautionary Motive: Poor readability increases financing costs, prompting cash hoarding.
  • Application in Study: Less readable disclosures exacerbate agency conflicts and financing constraints.
  • Empirical Validation: Results align with both theories.
  • Competing Theories: Stewardship theory (not supported).

5. Data Source Summary¶

  • Temporal Coverage: 1994–2017.
  • Sample Selection: Non-financial/utility firms with 10-Ks on EDGAR.
  • Data Cleaning: Winsorized at 1%/99%; handled missing R&D as zero.
  • Limitations: Survivorship bias; U.S.-only focus.

6. Key Empirical Findings¶

  • Statistical Significance: FOG coefficients significant at 1% level.
  • Economic Significance:
  • 1 SD ↑ FOG → $13.9–17.4M ↑ cash holdings for average firm.
  • 1 SD ↑ FOG → 14.6% ↓ dividends.
  • Cross-study Comparison: Consistent with Ertugrul et al. (2017) on financing costs.
