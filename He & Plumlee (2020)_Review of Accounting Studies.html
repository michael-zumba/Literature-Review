1. Literature Review Components¶

  • Title: Measuring Disclosure Using 8-K Filings
  • Journal: Review of Accounting Studies
  • Authors: Jing He & Marlene A. Plumlee
  • In-text Citation Format: He & Plumlee (2020)
  • Overview: This paper constructs and analyzes four voluntary and two mandatory disclosure measures using 8-K filings from 2005–2016. It examines their properties, correlations, and associations with firm characteristics.
  • Key Contribution to Field: Provides comprehensive, replicable 8-K-based disclosure proxies that address limitations of prior measures (e.g., single-channel bias, limited coverage). Introduces word-count-based measures as superior proxies.
  • Policy/Practical Implications: Informs regulators about disclosure practices and aids researchers in selecting appropriate measures. Highlights the interplay between voluntary and mandatory disclosures.
  • Methodology:
    • Data: 260,880 firm-quarters of 8-K filings from EDGAR.
    • Measures:
    • Voluntary: Count of 8-Ks (8K_Ct); count/word count of voluntary items (VDisc_Ct, VDisc_WC); sum of four disclosure channels (VDisc_Sum).
    • Mandatory: Count/word count of mandatory items (MDisc_Ct, MDisc_WC).
    • Analyses: Univariate statistics, correlations, quintile sorts, multivariate regressions.
  • Results:
    • High correlations among voluntary measures, but each captures unique aspects.
    • VDisc_WC (word count) is the superior proxy due to finer information granularity.
    • Voluntary disclosures are sticky and increase with firm size, analyst following, and concurrent mandatory disclosures.
    • Non-linear associations with performance (e.g., hump-shaped relation with ROA).
  • Discussion:
    • 8-K-based measures mitigate survivorship bias and capture broader disclosure channels.
    • Mandatory disclosures positively correlate with voluntary disclosures but are less persistent.
  • Research Gaps/Areas for Improvement:
    • Limited to U.S. firms; international comparisons needed.
    • Does not assess market reactions to disclosures.
  • Paper's Strengths and Weaknesses:
    • Strengths: Large sample, replicable methodology, concurrent mandatory disclosure controls.
    • Weaknesses: Relies on firms’ self-reported 8-K classifications; excludes non-8-K disclosures.
  • Limitations and Future Research:
    • Extend to non-8-K channels (e.g., press releases).
    • Explore nonlinearities in disclosure-performance links.

2. Variables Summary¶

  • Dependent Variable(s):
    • Voluntary Disclosure Measures:
    • 8K_Ct: Count of 8-K filings per quarter.
    • VDisc_Ct: Count of voluntary items (2.02, 7.01, 8.01) and exhibits.
    • VDisc_WC: Word count of voluntary items/exhibits.
    • VDisc_Sum: Sum of four channels (guidance, calls, non-GAAP, investor days).
    • Mandatory Disclosure Measures:
    • MDisc_Ct/MDisc_WC: Count/word count of mandatory items.
    • Data Source: SEC EDGAR.
  • Independent Variable(s):
    • Firm characteristics (size, profitability, litigation risk, etc.).
    • Data Source: Compustat, I/B/E/S, CRSP.
  • Control Variables:
    • Lagged disclosure measures, industry/year fixed effects, Herfindahl index (competition).

3. Methodology Summary¶

  • Analytical Methods: OLS regressions, correlation analysis, quintile sorting.
  • Identification Strategy: Natural experiment (SEC filing requirements).
  • Robustness Tests: Alternative measures (count vs. word count), subsample analyses.
  • Data Sampling: 10,290 firms, 2005–2016 (48 quarters).
  • Software/Tools Used: Python for data extraction, Stata for analysis.

4. Theory Summary¶

  • Name of Theory: Voluntary Disclosure Theory.
  • Origin: Verrecchia (1983), Dye (1985).
  • Description: Firms trade off proprietary costs and benefits (e.g., lower cost of capital) when disclosing.
  • Application in Study: Larger, more profitable firms disclose more; disclosures are persistent.
  • Competing Theories: Agency theory (mandatory disclosures as monitoring mechanism).

5. Data Source Summary¶

  • Primary Data: SEC EDGAR (8-K filings).
  • Supplementary Data: Compustat (financials), I/B/E/S (analysts), CRSP (returns).
  • Temporal Coverage: 2005–2016.
  • Sample Size: 260,880 firm-quarters.
  • Limitations: Excludes non-electronic filings pre-2005.

6. Key Empirical Findings¶

  • Economic Significance: 1 SD increase in firm size (LTAsst) raises VDisc_WC by 0.57 SD.
  • Cross-study Comparison: Consistent with Leuz & Schrand (2009) on 8-K counts but adds granularity.

7. Replication Materials¶

  • Data Availability: EDGAR, Compustat, CRSP (subscription required).
  • Code Repository: Not specified in paper.

8. Discipline-Specific Enhancements¶

  • GAAP/IFRS: Focuses on U.S. GAAP filings.
  • Corporate Governance: Links disclosure to board independence (Item 5.02).
